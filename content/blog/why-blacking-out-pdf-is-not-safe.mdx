## Quick answer

A black rectangle on a PDF **looks** final but usually **does not delete text**. Press **Ctrl+F** on a known hidden string — if search finds it, you have an overlay, not redaction. Safe workflows **remove or rasterize** content in marked areas, then verify on the **downloaded** file before you share. ## The mistake everyone makes

Security trainers see the same story: someone draws a black box in Edge, Preview, Word, or a free PDF site, emails the file, and assumes confidentiality. Recipients — or opposing counsel — run search and recover Social Security numbers, settlement figures, or patient identifiers in minutes. The visual cue of a black bar triggers the same instinct as redacted news photos. **PDFs do not work like printed paper with ink on top.**

## How PDFs store text (simplified)

PDFs are not single images. They contain instructions to draw text, embed fonts, place images, and add annotations. Drawing a filled black rectangle adds **new** instructions on top. Unless you delete the underlying text or rasterize the page, extractors read what is still there.

| Layer | Risk if you only draw black |
|-------|----------------------------|
| Content streams (text operators) | Ctrl+F and copy-paste still work |
| Fonts | Character codes recoverable |
| OCR text under scans | Invisible searchable text survives |
| Annotations | Separate from page body — easy to miss |

## "Redact" vs "black shape" in product marketing

Consumer apps optimize for speed. Marketing may say "redact" when the feature only **draws**. Professional tools **apply** redaction — removing or burning in content. Always read whether export deletes text operators or merely overlays shapes.

## What actually works

**Content stream removal** — deletes text objects in marked regions (verify anyway).

**Rasterization** — marked pages become bitmaps; text operators on those pages are gone. [RedactPDF](/tool) rasterizes marked areas on apply.

**Sanitization** — strips metadata, hidden layers, attachments — complements but does not replace visual redaction.

**Black shape only** — **unsafe** for confidential data.

## Scanned PDFs and OCR

Scanners often add an **invisible text layer** for search. Redacting only the bitmap while OCR text remains lets attackers copy hidden emails. Mark both layers or rasterize the full page after redaction — see [scanned PDF OCR guide](/blog/how-to-redact-scanned-pdf-ocr).

## Myths that still circulate

**Printing and re-scanning is always safe** — OCR may recover faint text; resolution matters.

**Password protection equals redaction** — passwords gate access; they do not remove content.

**Small boxes are safer** — size is irrelevant if text remains underneath.

## When are overlays acceptable?

Internal draft watermarks where everyone knows the file is **not** for external release. Never file overlays with courts or regulators as final.
